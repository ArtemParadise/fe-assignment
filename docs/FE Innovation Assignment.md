# **FE Innovation Assignment** 

**The project.** You'll find a small existing codebase. It works, but it wasn't built with much care — treat it as a piece of legacy code that has landed on your desk and now needs to be brought up to a standard you'd be comfortable owning. 

**Your task.** Review the project, form your own opinion of its strengths and weaknesses, and deliver a better, refactored version of it. What "better" means is deliberately left to your judgment — architecture, readability, correctness, performance, tooling, tests, developer experience: decide what matters most and act on it. 

**One boundary.** The design and existing features must stay as they are. Don't redesign the UI or add, remove, or alter functionality — unless a change genuinely improves the user experience, in which case use your judgment and be ready to explain the reasoning. This is a refactoring exercise, not a redesign. 

**How you work is up to you.** There are no restrictions on the approach, process, or tooling you use to get there. Use whatever gets you to the best result — we're just as interested in how you arrived at the solution as in the solution itself, so choose your methods deliberately and make the most of what's available to you. 

## **What to deliver.** 

1. The refactored code on GitHub — your improved version of the project. 

2. A report (free-form) that gives us an overview of your work: the main problems you found, the key changes you made and why, and the general process or flow you followed. Keep it high-level — you don't need to document every detail, since we'll walk through the finer points together in a follow-up conversation. Format is entirely your call; write it in whatever way best communicates your thinking. 

3. We're less interested in a "correct" answer than in your judgment, your standards, and the way you approach an open-ended problem. Have fun with it.
